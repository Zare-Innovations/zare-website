# Zare Website v3 — Final Founder-Level Gate

**Date:** 2026-08-06
**Status:** FINAL. Supersedes the strategy review where they conflict.
**Decision: GO. See Part 9.**

This document reverses my own previous NOT READY verdict and changes the build order. Both changes are explained rather than quietly made.

---

# PART 1 — DESTROYING THE PLAN

## 1.1 The most serious error: we designed for a buyer Zare does not have

Look at who actually pays Zare: Pickrell Global Technologies (technology services, Concord NC, $50–199k), Joveo (ad-tech, California), Kagool (consultancy), Exelia, KeySquare Labs, Medrafa, 251 Communications, AFIG Funds, CRDLE, 10.13 Enterprise.

**Not one of these is an enterprise with a procurement department running a vendor file.** These are SMB and mid-market companies between roughly 20 and 300 people. At that size, a $50–200k engineering engagement is bought by a founder or a VP Engineering who has budget authority and a hiring problem. There is no vendor-management function. There is no board packet.

Yet across three documents I specified: a procurement audience, data residency positioning, SLA language, and the phrase "I could put these people in front of my board."

**That is designing for the company Zare wants to become, not the company it is.** It is the single most damaging kind of error in a positioning project, because it makes everything read slightly aspirational, and an aspirational site from a 40-person company reads as overreach. A skeptical CTO smells it immediately.

**The correction is emphasis, not deletion.** The security content is excellent, cheap (it already exists), and does no harm. But it should not be a headline act. The real buyer's questions are more concrete and more urgent:

> "Can you get me two senior engineers in three weeks, will they be good, and will they still be here next year?"

That is the question the homepage must answer. Not "are you enterprise-compliant."

**Register shift:** less board-ready enterprise vendor, more *the team that will actually ship this for you*. Warmer, faster, more concrete. Still precise, but not institutional.

## 1.2 The positioning describes a structure, not a benefit

Current: *"A US-contracted engineering partner with an Addis Ababa delivery centre."*

Every word is true and defensible. But nobody wakes up needing a US-contracted partner. They wake up unable to hire fast enough. The statement describes how Zare is *organized*, not what a buyer *gets*.

I conflated two different jobs: the **category** Zare should own, and the **positioning statement** that explains it. Resolved in Part 2.

## 1.3 What a skeptical CTO would immediately distrust

| Element | Why it triggers doubt |
|---|---|
| `50-70% cost savings vs US hiring` | The widest claim on the page and the only one with no source. It also anchors on price, which is the position we say we're escaping. **A savings range as a top-line metric is the most outsourcing-website thing in the entire specification.** |
| `The next leap starts here.` | Zero information. Reads as a line chosen because it sounded good. |
| Six-metric grid at 132px | Visually loud, individually weak. Big numbers without sources read as decoration, and a CTO discounts the whole grid because one entry is unsourced. |
| `+50% above national average` framed as impact | To a client buyer this reads as CSR, and CSR from a vendor reads as deflection from a capability question. |
| "AI solutions" anywhere unattached to LitPulse | In 2026 this carries an automatic credibility discount. |
| A logo wall with no engagement context | The oldest trick in the outsourcing playbook. Logos without shape are assumed to be one-off or aspirational. |

## 1.4 Where it still feels like an outsourcing site wearing premium design

Three places, and they are all in the same failure mode: **leading with cost or scale rather than with work.**

1. The savings percentage as a headline metric.
2. `80+ placements in one month` — impressive throughput, but throughput is a *staffing* metric. It quietly reframes Zare as a supply channel rather than an engineering partner.
3. The "vetted talent" language, which is the standard vocabulary of every marketplace.

The antidote in all three cases is the same: **lead with the system that was built, not the people who were supplied.** LitPulse first, always.

## 1.5 Four visitors

**US CTO with a referral.**
Convinced by: the Clutch 5.0 verified February 2025; Pickrell running since January 2024; LitPulse being a genuinely non-trivial system.
Doubts remaining: who specifically would work on this; what happens when the engagement gets hard; whether the "senior" claim survives contact.
Leaves because: no architecture, no named engineer, and a savings percentage above the fold that makes them re-file Zare mentally under "cheap."

**Startup founder.**
Convinced by: speed (1–2 weeks to a candidate), the two clear paths, real faces.
Doubts: whether Zare will tell them what to build or just build what they say; whether a 40-person company will still care about a small account in six months.
Leaves because: the site talks about process and structure more than outcomes. Nowhere does it say what a business got, only what was delivered.

**Procurement manager.**
Convinced by: the Delaware entity, IP assignment, NDA-on-day-one, "no badges before they are real."
Doubts: almost none. Best-served visitor on the site.
Leaves because: they wouldn't. **But this person probably doesn't exist in Zare's current deal flow**, which is exactly the point of §1.1.

**Ethiopian engineer.**
Convinced by: LitPulse being real engineering; the +50% wage floor; the stack.
Doubts: will I do product work or maintenance; who would I learn from.
Leaves because: no engineer is named anywhere. The site sells engineers to clients but never shows one. To a candidate, that reads as exactly the body shop they're trying to avoid.

**The same gap disqualifies Zare with two of the four audiences: no named engineers.**

# PART 2 — QUESTIONING EVERY MAJOR DECISION

## 2.1 Positioning — resolved

Separate the two jobs.

**Category to own (one word, defended everywhere):** **embedded**.
Rejected: agency (commoditized), staff augmentation (lowest status), offshore/nearshore (price-anchored), freelance marketplace (no accountability), AI consultancy (credibility discount in 2026), "Ethiopian engineering advantage" (anchors on geography, which anchors on cost — actively dangerous).

**Positioning statement (what the site must make a stranger able to repeat):**

> **Senior engineers, embedded in your team, under US contracts, from our Addis Ababa engineering centre.**

Four verifiable facts, one adjective, no aspiration. "Senior" is backed by the eight-step vetting. "Embedded" is the category. "US contracts" is the structural moat. "Addis Ababa engineering centre" is stated with confidence rather than hedged.

**And the value proposition underneath it — the thing that actually wins deals:**

> **They stay.**

Every competitor category fails the same way: churn and discontinuity. Freelancers vanish, staff-aug rotates, agencies hand off, offshore shops reassign. Every piece of hard evidence Zare owns is continuity evidence — Pickrell since January 2024, 12+ months at Joveo, a verified 5.0 after two years of delivery.

**Retention is the product.** This reframes the strongest asset in the project: the +50% wage floor is not CSR, it is **the mechanism that produces retention**. Well-paid engineers do not leave. Stated that way it converts a soft impact claim into hard commercial evidence, and it works on the client and the candidate simultaneously.

That single reframe is the most valuable output of this review.

## 2.2 Homepage purpose — I overstated it

"Verification, not lead generation" was directionally right and rhetorically too absolute. The site does four jobs:

| Job | Weight | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Verification | 60% | Evidence density, scannability, third-party proof |
| Sales support | 15% | Shareable mid-conversation; sections that answer a specific question in isolation |
| Recruiting | 15% | Served by the same content. Costs nothing extra |
| Cold acquisition | 10% | SEO already ranks. Do not break it |

The **architecture** I specified was right. The **framing** was wrong, and if taken literally it would have justified weakening the conversion layer. It should not be weakened. Keep the forms, keep the two pathways, keep the response promise.

What remains true: no chat widget, no exit intent, no gated content, no popups. Those signal need, and need is the one thing a verification visitor must not sense.

## 2.3 Information architecture — reduced from ten to eight

Pushed hard on reduction. Two merges hold, and both make the content stronger rather than merely shorter.

**Merge A — "Security & IP" into "How an engagement works" → "Working with Zare."**
Both answer one question: *what are the terms of working together?* Commercial shape, timezone, ramp, legal, IP, security. Keeping them apart implied security is a separate concern; folding them in makes it a normal part of the operating model, which is a stronger message. `#security` survives as an anchor inside the section for procurement deep-links.

**Merge B — "People" and "Impact" → "The people, and why they stay."**
This is the merge that unlocks §2.1. Separately, People is a credibility section and Impact is a values section. Together they become **one argument about retention**: here are the named humans, here is the wage floor that keeps them, here is the founder. Impact stops being CSR and becomes evidence. Both audiences served at once.

**Result: 8 sections.**

Would a real CTO scroll all eight? No. Most form a judgment by section four and either leave or jump to Contact. **That is fine and expected** — sections 5 to 7 exist to survive the drill, not to carry the argument. But it means the page must be *winnable in four sections*, which drives the order below.

**Cannot reduce below 8.** Tested every further merge:
- Trust into Hero → the hero is already carrying maximum load.
- Paths into Hero → self-identification needs room; two funnels is the core IA fact.
- Work into Deliver → collapses evidence into process, which is backwards.
- Deliver into Working with Zare → capability and terms are different questions from different people; the merged section would be unreadable.

# PART 3 — DESIGN CRITIQUE

## 3.1 Is this "another Linear site"? Yes, at risk

Dark ground, Sora and Manrope, single teal accent, mono for data, restrained motion, evidence-first layout. That is the 2024–2026 house style of premium technical brands. On its own it is competent and derivative.

**Two things break the imitation, and I have been treating both as secondary:**

1. **Photographs of real people in Addis Ababa.** No Linear, Vercel, Stripe, or Raycast page contains a photograph of its team. It is the one visual move the reference set structurally cannot make, because their positioning depends on humans being invisible. Zare's product *is* people. **The photography is not decoration; it is the anti-imitation device.** It should be the largest visual element on the page after the hero, not a merged-away band.

2. **ዛሬ.** One Amharic word, used once. It cannot be copied and cannot be mistaken for anyone else.

**Decision: elevate photography from supporting asset to primary visual system.** Full-bleed, real, unstyled, in the office. This is also why the placeholder-mat pattern matters so much — where a real photograph does not exist yet, a labelled placeholder is more honest than stock, and honesty is the brand.

## 3.2 The hero — the weakest-justified expensive thing in the project

Attacking it properly.

**Against:** two of fifteen days, 13% of the entire budget, for one visual. Its meaning is deliberately hidden from visitors. WebGL heroes are saturated in 2026. It is the number-one schedule risk. A ~170 KB lazy chunk. **And no CTO has ever chosen a vendor because of a particle animation.**

**For:** the code already exists and is performance-engineered, so this is two days of *porting*, not creating. It is genuinely distinctive against competitor sites. And it makes an implicit argument that no copy can make: a company that ships a 14,000-particle GPU shader with reduced-motion handling and a WebGL fallback is a company that can write code.

**What should someone remember ten seconds later?** Not the Möbius. They should remember *senior engineers, US contracts, Addis Ababa, real clients*. The animation's job is to make them stay ten more seconds. It is a retention device, not a memory device.

**Would removing it make the site better?** No. But **the plan that builds it first is worse than the plan that builds it last.**

**Decision: keep it, cap it at 1.5 days, and move it to the end of the build.** Build every proof section first. If the hero slips or fails, the site ships complete with a static gradient and loses almost nothing. Under the current plan, a hero overrun on days 4 and 5 eats into the sections that actually win deals.

This is the most important scheduling change in this document.

## 3.3 Proof, ranked

**The three that create the most trust:**

1. **Clutch 5.0 verified, plus Pickrell running since January 2024.** Third-party attestation combined with duration. Nothing else on the site is in this class. **Currently the rating is unlinked** — an unlinked rating is an assertion.
2. **Named engineers with shipped work.** Absent everywhere. Disqualifying for two of four audiences. Most competitors structurally cannot do this.
3. **The LitPulse architecture diagram.** Converts the strongest asset from "they built a UI" to "they engineered a system."

**Distractions, in descending harm:**
- The `50-70%` savings figure as a headline metric. Anchors on price, unsourced, undermines the positioning. **Demote it out of the top-line grid.**
- `80+ placements in one month` presented as a top metric. Throughput is a staffing signal.
- Aggregate metrics generally. Six large numbers is a visually loud, individually weak block.
- The wage floor *framed as impact*. Reframed as retention evidence it moves into the top tier.

# PART 4 — ENGINEERING REALITY CHECK

**Appropriate and must not be compromised:**
Provider isolation into `(admin)`. Server-first with a handful of client islands. The `html.anim` gate. Type-level content invariants (a quote without attribution failing typecheck). Preserving the SEO layer and the careers system untouched. Wrapping `mobius.js` rather than porting to R3F.

**Over-engineered — delete:**
`scripts/build-map.ts`. A build script plus a TopoJSON dependency plus a generated module, for one section. Hand-export the SVG once. Saves half a day.
`<Magnetic>`. No API, no defined use. Remove from the tree.
Fifteen primitives before any page exists. Build eight on day 2; add the rest as sections demand them.

**Under-engineered:**
No visual design step. Ten (now eight) sections with no comps produces eight subtly inconsistent layouts, which reads as exactly the template site we are trying not to build. The CTO prototype covers roughly six sections at high fidelity, so design-in-browser is viable — but it costs about 20% on the section-building days and must be planned, not absorbed.

**Technical risks, ranked:**

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hero overrun consuming proof sections | **Build the hero last.** Hard 1.5-day cap, static fallback ready |
| No comps → inconsistent layouts | Design in browser from the prototype; build the four unprototyped sections against explicit written layout rules |
| Content blockers arriving late | Every blocked slot has a documented fallback and is scheduled late |
| Build OOM at default heap | Expected to resolve with provider isolation; raise `NODE_OPTIONS` in the Dockerfile if not |
| Careers regression | Restyle only. The diff must contain no logic changes |
| Budget honesty | First-load ≤190 KB excludes the lazy hero chunk. Total interactive `/` is ~400 KB. **LCP must be the hero text, never the canvas** |

# PART 5 — CONTENT AND CLAIM AUDIT

**KEEP — unique, specific, self-limiting**
`No badges before they are real. Current status available on request.` · `Evidence, not adjectives.` · `One team. Embedded in yours.` · `Eight steps between an application and your team.` · `A stack chosen for outcomes, not logos.` · `Dedicated placements, never shared.` · `Full IP assignment on all work product, in every contract.`

**REWRITE — right idea, weak expression**
`The next leap starts here.` → keep only if the eyebrow and sub-line carry all specificity. It is the one line on the site with no evidence behind it.
`Business growth that creates opportunity.` → reframe entirely as retention: *the wage floor is why our engineers stay*.
`50-70% cost savings vs US hiring` → demote out of the headline grid; state as a fact inside the engagement section where it belongs, with its basis.
`Built by people who understand both technology and opportunity.` → replace with named people. A photograph and three names outperform any sentence here.
LitPulse metrics → add one line of business outcome. `>40% full-text recovery` needs to say what that unlocked.

**DELETE**
Any unattached "AI solutions." Any unnamed client. Any adjective without a verifiable noun. `80+ placements in one month` as a *top-line* metric (keep it inside the embedded-teams track where throughput is the relevant question). Every banned word. "BPO" and "outsourcing" outside a meta description.

## The Zare Website Writing Rules

1. **Prefer a sentence that limits a claim over one that expands it.** Limiting language reads as honesty; expansive language is discounted to zero by this audience.
2. **Every number carries its source, or renders as a labelled placeholder.** A visible gap makes every other number credible.
3. **Name the client, or delete the claim.**
4. **Adjectives attach to verifiable nouns.** Not "seamless integration" but "in your codebase, in your standups, on your permissions."
5. **Never describe what can be shown.** Show the architecture; let the reader conclude the capability.
6. **Lead with the system built, never with the people supplied.**
7. **Never lead with cost.** Cost is a fact stated once, late, in context. It is never a headline.
8. **Write for scanning, reward drilling.** Every heading legible alone; depth for the one section they actually read.
9. **No banned words. No em dashes.** Enforced in CI, not in review.

# ADDENDUM — EXECUTION CONSTRAINTS (accepted at gate approval)

Five constraints raised at approval, accepted with three refinements. These are binding on implementation.

## A1 — Case studies read as technical postmortems, not brochure entries

The failure mode for S4 is a perfectly designed empty shell. The required structure:

```
Problem      what was actually broken, in the client's terms
System       the components, named
Decisions    why this stack, what tradeoffs were accepted
Outcome      what changed for the business
```

**Refinement — split across two surfaces.** The full postmortem is roughly 400 words and would bury the other two cases on the homepage.

- **Homepage S4 card:** problem, system shape, outcome. Six lines. Routes to the detail page.
- **`/work/[slug]`:** the full postmortem including the Decisions block. This is where a CTO is convinced.

`CaseStudy` gains two fields:
```ts
system: { component: string; note: string }[];   // named parts of the built system
decisions: { choice: string; rationale: string; tradeoff: string }[];
```
`decisions` renders on the detail page only. Target: the visitor thinks *these people think like engineers, not vendors*.

## A2 — Named engineers outrank the hero

Confirmed priority order, and it changes nothing in the build order because the build order already reflects it:

1. Work evidence · 2. Engineers · 3. Client proof · 4. Engagement model · 5. Hero

Required card shape:
```
[Photograph]
[Full name]
[Title]
Built:      [system or component]
Stack:      [4 to 5 technologies]
With Zare:  [year joined]
```

**Unflagged risk — this is a poaching surface.** Name, photo, stack, tenure, and shipped work is directly actionable by a recruiter or by a client who would rather hire than contract.

**Recommendation: publish full names.** Hedging to a first name and last initial undercuts the retention argument the entire site is built on. If the wage floor works, publishing names is the proof it works. If the fear of poaching is real, the retention claim is not.

**This is a founder decision, not an engineering one.** Fallback if declined: first name plus last initial, no LinkedIn link, and the section loses most of its value.

## A3 — "They stay" is the spine, used sparingly

Accepted as the organizing idea. **Refinement: three load-bearing placements, not eight.** A phrase in every section becomes a tic and stops being read.

| Placement | Line |
|---|---|
| Hero sub-line | Engineers who stay with your product, not rotate through your account. |
| S6 headline | How we build continuity. |
| S7 headline | The people, and why they stay. |

**Navigation labels stay conventional.** `Our Work`, not "Systems we stayed with." A scanning executive needs predictable wayfinding; creativity belongs in headlines, not in nav. Elsewhere the theme is carried by *evidence* — engagement durations on logos, tenure on engineer cards, the wage floor stated as mechanism.

The site quietly answers one question throughout: *will these people still care in six months?*

## A4 — "Embedded" must be defined on first use

"Embedded engineering" collides with *embedded systems* for exactly the technical audience being targeted. Category word retained (established, implies duration, one block resolves it), but it is defined before it is used.

Placed first in the S3 Embedded Teams panel, above all other content:

> **Embedded means your engineers work inside your workflow.** Your Slack, your standups, your codebase, your roadmap. They remain part of a stable Zare engineering team, with the management, review, and continuity that implies.

Without this, buyers file Zare next to Toptal and Upwork, which is the wrong comparison set and the wrong price anchor.

## A5 — M0 confirmed as first milestone

The real risks are leaked credentials, no analytics baseline, architecture drift, and planning paralysis. Not missing logos.

**Implementation note on provider isolation:** moving `MantineProvider` and the Redux `Provider` out of the root layout does **not** reduce the marketing bundle on its own, because the current marketing pages use Mantine throughout. M0 moves the providers *down* into `(marketing)` and the two admin layouts. That is the enabling refactor: the root bundle stops being shared, and the new `(site)` route group is then built without them. The bundle win lands when sections are rebuilt, not on day one. M0 acceptance is measured on structure, not on bundle size.

## A6 — Launch success metrics

Added to the specification. The site's job is to create confidence before a sales conversation, not to win awards.

**Primary, first 90 days:** qualified conversations generated from the website.

**Definition, because otherwise it is unmeasurable:** an inbound inquiry that reaches a scoping call. Tracked manually in a sheet with source, pathway, and date. Roughly 20 rows a quarter. This is not a CRM project.

**Secondary:**

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| `/work` and `/work/[slug]` engagement (visits, time, depth) | Tests whether the proof surfaces do the convincing |
| Contact start rate, and start-to-completion rate | Separates "no interest" from "form friction" |
| Careers applications | The recruiting objective |
| Returning visitors | Verification behaviour: they came back to check |
| Homepage scroll depth by section | Where the argument loses people |

**Explicitly not tracked as success:** visitors, impressions, session duration on `/`, animation completion.

**Baseline capture is required before any visual change ships.** After launch it is unrecoverable.
